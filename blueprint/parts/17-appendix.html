C.2 AI CMO system prompt — v1.0

Version-controlled, hashed, and referenced by agents.config.prompt_version. Changing one word creates a new version and requires the golden set to pass before promotion.

You are the Chief Marketing and Growth Officer of AI-MOS.

MISSION
Help the business reach measurable growth through marketing, lead generation,
sales and retention — at the lowest sustainable operating cost.

OBJECTIVES, IN ORDER
1 Generate qualified leads.
2 Increase conversion of leads to customers.
3 Reduce acquisition cost per won customer.
4 Reduce manual work per outcome.
5 Increase retention and recurring revenue.

PRINCIPLES
- Optimise for business outcome per unit of fully-loaded cost, not for task count.
- Understand the objective before executing. If critical information is missing,
  return it in missing_information and stop. Never guess a price, a capability,
  a testimonial or a guarantee.
- Use the organisation Brand Brain as the only source of product facts.
- Decompose the objective into the smallest set of tasks that produces the outcome.
- Assign each task to the role whose manifest permits it; never invent a role.
- Prefer the cheapest model tier that meets the required quality. Escalate on
  impact, uncertainty or a failed evaluation — never on the agent's name.
- Never duplicate work. Check existing tasks, leads and campaigns before creating.
- Define a success metric for every task you create.

TASK EXECUTION
1 Restate the desired outcome.
2 Identify the audience and the segment.
3 Load the relevant Brand Brain passages.
4 List missing information; stop if it blocks execution.
5 Produce a plan conforming to the ExecutionPlan schema.
6 Attach dependencies so that nothing runs before its input exists.
7 Estimate cost using the registry prices; include data and sending where relevant.
8 Mark every task that requires approval, and say which approval.
9 State the risks that would make this plan fail.

PERMISSIONS — HARD CONSTRAINTS
- Never bypass approval. Never spend money. Never set or change a price.
- Never commit to terms, discounts or refunds.
- Never claim an action succeeded without a tool result confirming it.
- Respect suppression and consent without exception.
- Escalate sensitive, disputed or legally consequential conversations.

OUTPUT
Return only the ExecutionPlan object. No prose outside the schema.
If the plan cannot be produced, return final_status: blocked with the reason.

SUCCESS
The best measurable business result at acceptable cost and acceptable risk —
not the largest number of tasks.

Prompt version 1.0 · hash recorded in the agent manifest · evaluation set evals/cmo_golden.jsonl · owned by Product · reviewed at each phase exit. This prompt constrains behaviour; it does not enforce it. Enforcement is Part 3.4.

Appendix F

Reality check and
corporate path

Everything above is buildable. That is a different claim from "this will sell any product to any market", and the difference decides whether the project is worth starting.

F.1 What is genuinely realistic here

Nothing in Parts 1–14 depends on a research breakthrough or an API that does not exist. Every integration is a documented public API; every phase has an exit that can be demonstrated in an afternoon; every component is sized for a small team working in sequence rather than for an organisation running in parallel. The engineering risk is ordinary: integration quirks, deliverability, data quality, and the discipline of keeping a system honest about what it actually did.

The risk that matters is not technical. It is that the parts of the plan which are not code — the offer, the list, the sending reputation, the daily approval habit — are the parts that decide whether anything works.

F.2 Four claims from the original brief that do not survive contact

The brief impliedWhat is actually trueConsequence for the plan
The whole platform in roughly twelve weeksA first usable slice in that order of effort; the full 23-module surface over several months, with two of those months spent on integrations and deliverability rather than features.Gates replace dates (Part 11). Only PH-0 through PH-3 matter for the first proof.
Twenty agents is the productTwenty roles are configuration. The durable advantages are the approval workflow, the accumulated brand and campaign data, the send reputation, and the cost ledger.Build one engine. Ship ten roles. Treat the rest as later releases.
Leads arrive with verified contact dataMatch rates vary by provider, region and seniority; some records will be stale. Verification is an event, not a property.Every record carries source and confidence (Part 7). Never promise a verified list.
AI does the marketingAI does the execution. Positioning, pricing, offer design and the decision to keep going remain human, and are the reason campaigns succeed or fail.Brand Brain is human-written on purpose (PH-0, T-0.7).

F.3 The three gates that decide whether it works

None of them is software. All three can be measured from the first week of real sending, and any one of them can end the venture regardless of code quality.

GateWhat it actually isMetric that proves itHow it fails
DeliverabilityWhether your messages reach an inbox at all, and whether that stays true as volume grows.Bounce < 2%, complaint < 0.1%, inbox placement on seed tests.One careless campaign on a cold domain — then the channel is gone and takes weeks to rebuild.
Data and lawful basisWhether the people you contact are real, relevant and lawfully contactable.Valid-contact rate, opt-out rate, reply rate per source.Buying volume instead of accuracy. Reply rates collapse and reputation follows.
Offer and fitWhether the message describes something a stranger wants at that price.Reply rate, meeting rate, lead-to-customer conversion.Nothing in this system fixes a weak offer. It will just deliver rejection more efficiently.

F.4 Can this sell "any product"? No — and the distinction is useful

The engine removes the labour bottleneck from a growth motion that already works. It does not create demand, and it does not substitute for product-market fit. Used honestly, that is still an extremely valuable thing to own.

This system wins whenThis system loses when
The niche is narrow and the ICP can be written in one paragraph.The product needs a demo before it can be understood.
Deal size is low to mid, so volume and a short cycle matter more than relationships.Procurement, legal review and six stakeholders decide the purchase.
The offer is explainable in a short email and provable in a first call.Trust must be established before any conversation is possible.
Someone will approve sends daily and read a weekly digest.Nobody has twenty minutes a day to review output.
The market is not already saturated with automated cold outreach.The inbox is already full of identical AI-written pitches.
A partner agency resells it across client workspaces.Every customer needs a bespoke integration before value.
The positioning that survives scrutiny

Not "an AI sales team". That framing invites the question "then why do I need you", and it collapses the first time a campaign underperforms. The defensible framing is: an execution engine for a growth motion you have already proven, with governance and cost visibility built in. It promises throughput and control, not judgement.

F.5 Corporate readiness — what a future enterprise sale actually requires

This architecture is already shaped for it: tenant isolation is a database guarantee, every action is audited, every cost is attributed, every provider sits behind an adapter. What is missing is almost entirely non-AI work, and it should be budgeted as such.

The test to apply before spending anything

Can you show ten real emails that a real prospect replied to, on a domain that still delivers, with the cost of those ten visible? Until that is true, there is no product — only a plan. Every phase before PH-3 exists to make that sentence true as cheaply as possible.
