Part 12

Economics

This part contains illustrative numbers. They exist to show the shape of the calculation and the relative weight of each cost line — not to be quoted to a customer or a board without re-measurement.

12.1 Anatomy of one acquisition

Cost per qualified lead is the sum of five lines. Only one of them is inference.

Cost linePer 100 leadsShareDriven by
Prospect data and enrichment$8 – $4040 – 60%Provider price per record, match rate, re-enrichment policy.
Sending infrastructure$3 – $1515 – 30%Mailboxes, ESP plan, domain warm-up time.
Human review$2 – $2010 – 30%Minutes per approved batch × hourly rate. Often the largest controllable line.
Model inference$0.02 – $0.30under 2%Task count × tokens × tier. At founder volume this is the smallest line by an order of magnitude.
Infrastructure amortised$0.50 – $21 – 5%VPS, database, object storage, monitoring.

Worked example (illustrative). 100 discovered prospects → 60 qualified → 55 delivered → 6 replies → 2 meetings → 1 customer. At $25 data, $8 sending, $10 review, $0.15 inference and $1 infrastructure, fully-loaded cost per won customer is about $44 and cost per qualified lead about $0.74. Change the reply-to-meeting rate and the same campaign costs $90 or $30 per customer — which is why the instrumented funnel matters more than the token price.

The number that governs pricing

Cost per won customer, not cost per lead. A campaign can look cheap per lead and be ruinous per customer if qualification is loose. Every campaign screen must show both, and the second must be computed from verified payments.

12.4 The pricing question, stated properly

Three viable models, with the constraint each imposes on the build:

ModelFits which customerRequires from the build
Seat subscriptionAgencies, teamsRoles, invitations, per-seat limits, and usage caps to protect margin.
Usage-based (per lead, per campaign)SaaS foundersAccurate, per-unit cost attribution — which this architecture provides by design.
Flat with a workflow capEarly adoptersHard budget enforcement (Part 5.4) and a visible overage path.

The architecture deliberately makes usage-based pricing possible: every action carries tenant, campaign, task and cost. Whether the business chooses to charge that way is a commercial decision, but it should not be foreclosed by a schema that cannot attribute cost.

12.5 The operating cost target

For a founder running one product at low volume, inference should cost under $1 per month. That is not optimism — it follows from the unit price of a task, and it is worth stating as a target because it changes what you optimise.

Work at 200 leads / monthUnitVolumeMonthly
Classification, extraction, scoring (T1)$0.0002~400$0.08
Drafting, replies (T1)$0.0002~250$0.05
Planning and strategy (T2)$0.002~25$0.05
Total inference≈ $0.16

Default envelopes, set in spec/ai-mos.spec.yaml#budgets and enforced before dispatch: $0.02 per task, $0.50 per workflow run, $0.10 per agent per day, $2.00 per organisation per month. The ceiling exists to stop a runaway loop, not to authorise spending — normal use sits an order of magnitude below it.

Two honest caveats

Credit is sold in blocks. Providers top up in $5 or similar increments, so the card moves in steps even when usage is cents. A $0.16 month still debits $5 once.

Inference is not your fixed cost. Once hosted, infrastructure is $40–200 per month — the thing that actually dominates a quiet month. If the target is genuinely "under $1 in total", that means running the stack locally in Docker through PH-3 and deploying only when there is a customer paying for it. The plan supports that: nothing in PH-1 to PH-3 requires a server.

The number to watch is not the monthly invoice but cost per qualified lead — and at these prices inference contributes less than a cent to it. If your CPL is $0.74, model spend is 0.2% of the story and the other 99.8% is data, sending and your own review time.

13.2 Decisions taken by this document

Each decision is recorded with the alternative that was rejected, so it can be reopened deliberately rather than by accident.

ADR-01Payment provider chosen at PH-0, behind an adapterAssume a global provider worksEligibility is jurisdictional and can invalidate the revenue loop.
ADR-02Seven services, twenty-three modulesOne service per moduleMost modules are views over shared tables; service count drives operational cost.
ADR-03RLS in the database from migration 01Application-level tenancy filtersA single missed filter is a breach; the database cannot forget.

ADR-05OpenRouter as the single model gatewayDirect provider SDKsModel choice becomes configuration and cost attribution becomes trivial.
ADR-06Model identifiers are registry data, never codeHardcoded model stringsModel churn is certain; a string in code makes it a migration.

ADR-08Approvals bound to a payload hash, single-useApprove a campaign once, send laterOtherwise approval of 200 becomes authorisation of 2,000.
ADR-09Payment state written only by verified webhookTrust a checkout redirect or agent inferenceMoney must be a fact, not an interpretation.
ADR-10No scraping, no access-control bypass, in any phaseAggressive acquisition tacticsChannel bans destroy the product; the commercial risk exceeds the gain.
