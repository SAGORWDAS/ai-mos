How to read this document

Each part answers one question, and each part is meant to be read at a different moment in the build. Part 1 is read once. Part 6 stays open on the desk. Part 11 is the working plan. Appendix A is ingested by tooling, not read by people.

PartRead it whenQuestion it answers
1–2Onboarding anyoneWhat is this product, and what are its objects?
3–5Before writing infrastructure codeHow does the system hold together?
6–10Continuously, per moduleWhat exactly does each module do and own?
11Every working dayWhat do I build next, and what proves it done?
12–13Before spending moneyWhat does it cost, and what could go wrong?
14At every phase exitIs it actually finished?
A–FOnce, and before committing budgetMachine contracts, schemas, glossary, references — and an honest assessment of what this cannot do.

Normative language

KeywordMeaning in this document
MUSTNon-negotiable. Violating it is a defect, not a design choice. Most MUST rules concern security, tenancy, money or data integrity.
MUST NOTA prohibited behaviour. Every MUST NOT has a matching test in Part 14.
SHOULDThe default. Deviating requires a one-line recorded justification in the decision log (Part 13.2).
MAYGenuinely optional, usually a later-phase enhancement.
IllustrativeNumbers, prices, model identifiers and capacity figures marked illustrative are not contracts. Re-measure before relying on them.

The document in one sentence

One multi-tenant backend owns every object and every permission; one agent runtime executes configured roles against a fixed tool gateway; and a seven-phase sequence delivers one provable business outcome per phase — beginning with ten real emails, not with a dashboard.
