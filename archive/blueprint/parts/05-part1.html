Part 1

Product definition

AI-MOS is not a content generator with a CRM attached. It is a system that owns a growth objective end to end — from finding a stranger to collecting money from them — and reports what that cost.

1.1 What the product actually is

A business owner states an outcome. The system produces a plan, decomposes it into tasks, assigns those tasks to configured agent roles, executes only what its permissions allow, stops for approval where the risk requires it, and records the result against a business metric.

"Find 200 US marketing agencies that fit our ICP, research them, write personalised
 outreach, get my approval, send it, handle the replies, and book demos."

That sentence is the entire product. Everything in the 23-module navigation is an instrument for executing some part of it, and everything in this blueprint exists so that sentence can be run repeatedly, cheaply, and without embarrassing the business.

Three properties separate this from a chat interface wrapped around an email API:

PropertyWhat it means operationally
StatefulA lead, a campaign and a conversation exist as durable records with histories. Closing the browser does not end the work, and re-opening it does not restart it.
GovernedAutonomy is a per-agent, per-action configuration, not a global switch. Every external effect passes a validator, a budget check and — where required — a human.
AccountableEvery task carries its model, tokens, latency and cost; every external action carries a result confirmation and an audit entry. Cost per qualified lead is a query, not an estimate.

1.2 The five outcomes, and how each is instrumented

The product is judged on five business outcomes. Each one is deliberately paired with the database field that proves it, because an outcome you cannot query is a slogan.

#OutcomeInstrumented asSource
1More qualified leadsCount of leads in state qualified per campaign per week, with the scoring model version attached.ENT-leads
2More sales conversations and conversionsCount of conversations with a prospect reply; count of meetings booked; count of customers created from a lead.ENT-conversations, ENT-meetings
3Lower acquisition cost(data + sending + ad spend + review labour + inference) ÷ won customers, computed per campaign.ENT-model_usage, ENT-payments
4Less manual workRatio of agent-completed tasks to human-touched tasks; median minutes of human review per approved campaign.ENT-tasks, ENT-approvals
5Better retentionActive subscriptions at period end; churn events attributed to a reason code; retention-agent interventions that preceded a renewal.ENT-subscriptions
Design consequence

Because outcome 3 must be computable per campaign, cost attribution cannot be bolted on later. Every model call MUST carry a campaign_id (nullable), a task_id and an organization_id from the first migration. This single requirement shapes the schema in Part 7 more than any other.

1.3 Who it is for, and in what order

Four segments were proposed. Only two have the combination this product needs: a short sales cycle, an outcome that is measurable within weeks, and a customer who already buys marketing software. Build for those two. The others arrive when the engine is proven, not before.

SegmentPriorityWhy this position
SaaS founders / small software companiesPrimaryClear ICP, measurable pipeline, willingness to run an assistant that emails on their behalf. The demonstration case in Part 14 is built on this segment.
Digital marketing agenciesPrimaryThe only segment that pays for seats rather than for outcomes, and can run several client workspaces — which forces multi-tenancy to be real early.
SMB servicesSecondaryHigh volume, low budget, lower technical tolerance. Needs a simpler onboarding path that does not exist until PH-5.
E-commerceLaterValues creative and paid channels over 1:1 sales conversations, which is the part of this system that is hardest to build and easiest to prove. Wrong first fight.
Consultants and solo service providersLaterReal need, but the economics are thin and the volume is low; they are a by-product of a working SaaS offering, not a target.

1.4 The MVP boundary

The first release is defined as much by exclusion as by inclusion. Anything not listed on the left is out, even if it appears in the 23-module navigation — those modules exist as navigation targets in the information architecture, not as PH-1 obligations.

In the first release

  1. Command Center (MOD-02)
  2. AI Manager / orchestration (MOD-03)
  3. Agent registry (MOD-04)
  4. Brand Brain (MOD-18)
  5. Approval Center (MOD-20)
  6. Research agent (AGT-02)
  7. Strategy agent (AGT-03)
  8. Content agent (AGT-06)
  9. Lead generation + qualification (AGT-04, AGT-05)
  10. Outreach agent (AGT-07)
  11. Sales agent (AGT-08)
  12. Analytics agent (AGT-10)
  13. OpenRouter gateway + budgets (Part 5)
  14. Gmail send and inbox read (INT-02)

Explicitly out of the first release

  • Paid advertising execution (read-only reporting only)
  • Social publishing
  • SEO tooling
  • Voice, WhatsApp, SMS channels
  • Marketplace or partner programme
  • White-label / reseller billing
  • Any agent at autonomy L4
  • Any autonomous price, refund or contract action
  • Native mobile applications
  • Data enrichment from scraped sources

The exclusions are not a statement of unimportance. They are a statement about what a small team can make trustworthy. A system that sends ten correct, approved, individually-written emails is more valuable than one that can claim to run advertising.

1.5 Metrics, and the anti-metrics

Every metric below is computed from platform data, never entered by hand. Each is paired with a metric that is deliberately not used as evidence of success — usually because it is easy to inflate and weakly correlated with revenue.

MetricDefinitionWhy it is the one that mattersNot a substitute for it
CPLFully-loaded cost ÷ qualified leads deliveredIncludes data, sending and inference, so it cannot be flattered by cheaper tokens.Cost per email sent
CACFully-loaded acquisition cost ÷ new paying customersThe only number a founder can compare against gross margin.Cost per meeting booked
Reply rateHuman replies ÷ delivered messages, excluding bounces and auto-respondersA real human response is the first honest signal of market interest.Open rate (unreliable by design — see 1.5.1)
ConversionCustomers created ÷ qualified leadsSeparates a lead-quality problem from a sales-message problem.Click-through rate
MRR / churnVerified from payment-provider events onlyRevenue is not an inference. See the payment rule in Part 9.Self-reported deal value
$/taskModel cost of one completed agent task, by task typeTurns "AI is expensive" into a number you can act on.Monthly token spend
Escalation rateTasks routed to a human ÷ total tasks, by typeThe honest measure of how autonomous the system actually is.Task count completed
1.5.1 The open-rate trap

Email privacy proxies prefetch images and generate opens that no human performed. Any dashboard that reports open rate as a headline metric will eventually report success on a campaign that nobody read. Track opens for diagnostics only, label them as unreliable in the UI, and never let them drive an automated decision such as escalating a lead's score.

1.6 Non-goals

Stated plainly, because each of these will be proposed again in month three by someone reasonable:

  • Not a general-purpose agent platform. Only roles that serve the five outcomes exist. No plugin marketplace, no arbitrary user-authored agents in v1.
  • Not a data broker. Prospect data comes from permitted providers and first-party sources. No scraping of platforms whose terms prohibit it, and no bypassing access controls (Part 9.1).
  • Not a replacement for a CRM in an enterprise. It is a growth engine that exports. HubSpot and equivalents are integrations (P1), not competitors to be rebuilt.
  • Not an autonomous money-spender. No agent may move money, change a price, issue a refund or commit to terms at any phase in this document.
  • Not a chat product. The Command Center is how an objective is entered; it is not where the work is observed. Work is observed in tasks, approvals and metrics.

1.7 The constraint that governs every decision

This is built by a very small team. That is not a footnote — it is the reason this document collapses 23 modules into 7 services, defers 9 of 20 agent roles, and refuses Kubernetes, a service mesh and anything that needs a dedicated operations engineer.

ConstraintDesign rule that follows from it
One part-time engineerEvery component must be operable by a person who reads the logs directly. One database, one queue, one deploy target.
No dedicated SREManaged Postgres if affordable; otherwise one well-tuned instance with tested backups and a documented restore.
Cash-constrainedFixed monthly infrastructure before variable inference. Cost per workflow must be knowable before a workflow is launched at scale.
Reputational riskOne badly sent campaign can cost the founder their domain. Approval, suppression and rate limits are P0, not polish.
